        Scientific use

        These plots contain no smoothing, fitting, or interpolated physics. Curves connect direct evaluations of the implemented equations only for readability. A visually smooth trend does not establish model validity, identify a wave mode, or replace uncertainty analysis. Exported files record the displayed values and fixed state.

        Interpretation

        A smoke test confirms that a calculator executes on its valid default inputs; it is not an independent numerical benchmark. Passing all checks confirms implementation consistency for the tested definitions, but it does not turn reduced formulas, empirical fits, or simplified instability criteria into general kinetic solvers. Each calculator states its own scope.

        About the project

        A transparent formulary for space-plasma work.

        Alfvenica is an independent, open-source browser toolkit that brings commonly used space- and astrophysical-plasma calculations together with their units, assumptions, interpretation, and sources. It is intended for researchers, educators, and learners who want to inspect the model behind a number rather than use a black-box result.

        Why I built it

        I began developing Alfvenica while working with spacecraft observations and kinetic-Alfvén-wave problems. Repeatedly checking units, conventions, assumptions, and limiting cases was often as important as obtaining the numerical result. The toolkit grew from that practical need.

        What it is for

        Alfvenica combines a searchable calculator, physical interpretation, controlled plotting, illustrative plasma states, explicit model assumptions, primary references, and an in-browser validation report. It supports first-pass estimates, teaching, consistency checks, and transparent methods—not automated wave identification.

        Design, units, and privacy

        Calculations and plots run locally through one canonical SI physics core; input values are not transmitted. Space, SI, and CGS choices are interface conversions only. Formula definitions, assumptions, references, interpretations, plots, and tests remain separated to reduce scientific drift.

        Scientific basis and validation

        Constants follow CODATA 2022. Foundational coefficients are checked against the 2023 NRL Plasma Formulary, while model-specific entries retain primary references and validity conditions. Reference benchmarks, analytical identities, domain checks, smoke tests, and interface checks are reported separately in Validation and the formula audit.

        Scope and limitations

        Single-ion, scalar-temperature, fluid, reduced two-fluid, bi-Maxwellian, and frozen-flow assumptions appear in several tools. Characteristic scales are usually ordering scales, not sharp universal boundaries. Alfvenica does not replace kinetic dispersion solvers, instrument pipelines, event context, or uncertainty analysis.

        Author and independence

        Alfvenica is created and maintained by Mani K Chettri, a final-year PhD candidate in Physics at Sikkim University (ORCID). It is independently developed; the affiliation identifies the author and does not imply institutional endorsement. No dedicated external funding supported this release.

        ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, DeepSeek, and Kimi assisted parts of coding, documentation, testing, and critical review. Their outputs were not accepted as scientific authority: formulas, assumptions, units, and numerical behaviour were checked against primary sources, analytical limits, and automated tests. Final responsibility for the scientific content and implementation remains with the author.
