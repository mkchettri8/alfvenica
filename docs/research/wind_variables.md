# Wind pilot variable dictionary (Pass 1)

Accessed 2026-09-27. **S** = NASA CDAWeb [WI_H1_SWE notes](https://cdaweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/misc/NotesW.html), [dataset DOI](https://doi.org/10.48322/nasd-j276), and the variable/global attributes of `wi_h1_swe_20200101_v01.cdf`. **M** = [WI_H0_MFI notes](https://cdaweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/misc/NotesW.html), [dataset DOI](https://doi.org/10.48322/av38-wn55), and the attributes of `wi_h0_mfi_20200101_v05.cdf`. **K** = [Kasper et al. (2006), equations (1)–(2)](https://wind.nasa.gov/docs/Kasper_SWE_Calibration_PaperJGR2006.pdf), which define the fitted most-probable thermal speed by `k_B T_j = m_j w_j²/2`. The [Wind ion data guide](https://wind.nasa.gov/data_sources.php) identifies H1 as the 92-second primary science product. Actual CDF file identities and hashes are in the [manifest](../../examples/wind_pilot/manifest.json).

All SWE rows share `Epoch`; the archive says it marks the **beginning** of the plasma spectrum, and its global `Time_resolution` is 92 seconds. The observed primary-window start separations are approximately 96.142 or 99.244 seconds, so cadence is not the same as integration support. SWE float fill is `-1e31`; `fit_flag` fill is `-128`. Internal Alfvenica units are m⁻³, tesla, energy-equivalent eV, m s⁻¹, and metres. Conversion uses the released core's proton mass and elementary charge, not a rounded temperature coefficient. Each row's archive `FILLVAL` and `VALIDMIN`/`VALIDMAX` must be read from its source CDF when replaying another version.

The [source-product notes](https://cdaweb.gsfc.nasa.gov/misc/NotesW.html) state that the archived H1 product already applies a minimum Mach number of 1.5, maximum fit chi-square/dof of 100000, minimum bow-shock distance of 5 Earth radii, and maximum fractional fitted-parameter uncertainty of 70%. These describe source-product screening, not additional Alfvenica filters. The proton parameters derive from bi-Maxwellian fits and cannot fully represent arbitrary beams, tails, nongyrotropy or an arbitrary distribution function.

The field and velocity vectors are GSE, but the four initial outputs use density, scalar or perpendicular temperature, and **field magnitude** only. Their numerical formulas are rotation invariant. Keep the GSE label so future vector operations cannot silently mix frames. The proton-only Alfvén speed uses `n_p m_p`; source-product alpha density is not folded into Alfvenica's single-species `mu` input.

The H1 `BX/BY/BZ` values are co-reported component means. Alfvenica takes the magnitude after those means; `|⟨B_vector⟩|` is not generally `⟨|B_vector|⟩`. Parallel thermal speed, alpha density, and `Ang_dev`/`dev` are present in the source product but are not imported or validated by the current analysis pathway, despite their entries in this source-variable inventory. Trace beta is neither parallel nor perpendicular beta and cannot replace an anisotropy or stability analysis.

The archive's `fit_flag` description does not imply every non-10 code corrupts proton quantities: codes 9/8/7/6/2 refer primarily to alpha complications, while codes 5/4/3 impose `T_p=T_alpha`, 1 is poor peak identification and 0 is a failed bimax fit. Retaining any non-10 class requires a separate justified sensitivity policy. The primary policy uses only 10, avoiding an invented equivalence among these contingencies. Source one-sigma columns are fit-derived and do not by themselves establish independent calibration uncertainty or covariance; see K, Appendix A.
